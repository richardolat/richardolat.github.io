Profile

The voice behind Ondo State's government, now advising leaders on what to say and when to say it.

Richard Olatunde served as Chief Press Secretary to the late Governor Oluwarotimi Akeredolu, SAN, from 2021 to 2023, the youngest person ever to hold the office in Ondo State. Before that he spent three years as Special Assistant on New Media and was the youngest spokesperson of a governorship campaign in the state, speaking for the Akeredolu/Aiyedatiwa ticket in 2020. He spoke for the government through a terror attack, a contested medical leave and a transition of power. Today he is Lead Strategist at Veritas-Eagles Communications and Services Ltd, leading brand, market and reputation work for companies and institutions well beyond politics.

    Case file 01

    The Statement That Turned a Crisis

    17 January 2023. A leaked voice note in the morning, a press statement before noon.

    Early on the morning of 17 January 2023, a voice note began to travel across Ondo State. The voice was attributed to the First Lady. In it she referred to a female Special Adviser to the Governor as "Iya Alagbo", alleging that she had been bringing agbo, a traditional herbal concoction, to treat the Governor. The recording also gave the impression that the Governor was gravely ill and unfit to discharge his duties. By mid-morning it was everywhere online, drawing heated comment. The questions it raised were no longer about who said what. People wanted to know the true state of the Governor and of his government.

    Silence would have handed the story to the rumour mill. A flat denial would have collapsed the moment the Governor next appeared in public looking unwell. Several senior cabinet members argued for exactly that: say nothing about his health, or deny everything.

    Richard Olatunde chose a third path, and he chose it fast. Before noon that same day, he issued a statement that did what no one in government had been willing to do. It admitted, in plain words, that the Governor had been ill and was receiving treatment.

    "Since the Governor is not a super human and the immunity his office enjoys does not extend to physical wellness or otherwise, the health issue is therefore not an unusual one."

    The statement was written to make the Governor human first and a public officer second. It acknowledged he was frail. It then set the record straight with a diary the public could check: the Executive Council and Security Council meetings of 11 January, the presidential campaign rally of 7 January, the Armed Forces Remembrance service of 8 January. It closed by thanking people for their prayers "even beyond partisan lines".

    The response turned on that honesty. Sympathy and goodwill messages came in from within the state and beyond it, and the conversation moved from the voice note to the Governor's recovery. Channels Television, Vanguard and ThisDay carried it the same day. With the central question answered by the government itself, the recording lost its power and the crisis it had started faded away.

    The record

    • The statement: "On Governor Akeredolu's State of Health, No Cause for Alarm"17 January 2023
    • Channels Television: Akeredolu recuperating, not bedridden17 January 2023
    • Vanguard: No cause for alarm, state government assures17 January 2023
    • ThisDay: Anxiety in Ondo over Akeredolu's health17 January 2023

    The approach

    • Disclose, do not deny
    • Humanise the principal
    • Prove capacity with a checkable diary
    • Thank the public and invite goodwill

    Case file 02

    Four Dates That Ended an Argument

    12 December 2023. A routine handover notice that answered a year of sponsored claims.

    Through 2023, as the Governor's health kept him away, Ondo State was gripped by a struggle over power. At its centre sat one claim, pushed through sponsored attacks and orchestrated commentary: that Governor Akeredolu did not hand over to his deputy when he left the state. The story cast him as a man clinging to office, and it was used to justify every move against him.

    On 12 December 2023, the Governor was set to begin a fresh medical leave. The announcement could have been a routine notice. Richard Olatunde wrote it to do two jobs at once. It stated that the leave would begin the next day, that a formal letter and notice transferring power would go to the House of Assembly in line with the Constitution, and that the Deputy Governor would act in his absence.

    Then it put the record on the table.

    "This practice was observed on April 1, 2021, April 1, 2022, April 3, 2023, and July 10, 2023, when he embarked on previous vacations."

    Four dates, each one checkable against the records of the House of Assembly. The statement did not name the accusers or argue with them. It placed the facts where anyone could test them. Once the dates were public, the claim that the Governor never let go of power had nothing left to stand on.

    The record

    • The statement: "Akeredolu Commences Medical Leave"12 December 2023
    • Channels Television: Akeredolu begins medical leave, transmits power to Aiyedatiwa12 December 2023

    The approach

    • Answer with records, not rhetoric
    • Make every claim checkable
    • Leave the accusers unnamed
    • Pair the announcement with the evidence

    Strategic Communications

    Brand, market and reputation work led at Veritas-Eagles Communications for companies, institutions and campaigns.

    Every brief starts with one question: what must this audience come to believe, and what evidence will earn that belief? The answer decides the message, the channel, the voice and the measure of success. The same method serves technology firms, corporate service providers, universities, public sector communicators and national campaigns. Each brief below was delivered by a team he leads.

    Technology · Market entry

    Taking a homegrown software brand to market

    An indigenous HR, payroll and accounting software company with two decades of professional services behind it needed buyers to see it as a serious alternative to foreign products. We built a twelve month market strategy around one goal: be the name a business owner finds first, even before they click.

    • Thought leadership and bylines in business publications
    • Search, LinkedIn, Google and Meta campaigns tied to cost per lead
    • Monthly live demos, webinars and chamber of commerce engagement
    • Referral and partner programme, plus WhatsApp and Slack communities
    • CSR through scholarships and SME support
    • A KPI framework tracking leads, conversion, branded search and retention

    Corporate services · Brand campaign

    Infinite Impact: a campaign for a facility management firm

    In a crowded and loosely regulated industry, a facility management company covering soft services, technical maintenance and FM administration wanted to be the first call for C-suite buyers. We created the campaign idea, the infinity loop identity and the TV script, then ran it across every layer of the market.

    • B2B targeting of decision-makers in maritime, oil and gas, telecoms, manufacturing, real estate, healthcare and finance
    • Television, radio, print and billboards in three major cities
    • Landing page, SEO, whitepaper, blog and LinkedIn polls
    • Conference sponsorship, client events and executive presentations
    • Guerrilla installations, urban murals and a youth maintenance training CSR
    • A 90 day rollout plan

    Education · Enrolment

    Growing a new university past its pioneer class

    A young private university had matriculated its first students and needed to turn that milestone into a steady enrolment pipeline. The message was simple and honest: we are new, but we are serious about quality. We carried it to students, parents and the teachers who advise them.

    • Content calendars for Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube
    • Courtesy visits and partnerships with radio stations across six states
    • Sponsored morning radio programmes, jingles and television features
    • Media relations aimed at earned coverage and leadership profiles
    • Education bloggers and parenting influencers as third party voices
    • Secondary school visits, career talks and host community CSR

    Public sector · Training

    Training the press secretaries of a state's local councils

    We designed and ran a two day capacity programme for the chief press secretaries of every local council in a state. It was built from what he learnt in the job: how to turn policy data into stories people feel, and how to hold a line when the environment is hostile.

    • Turning development and policy reports into citizen stories
    • AI tools for social listening, sentiment and rapid response
    • Crisis detection, counter-messaging and a live crisis simulation
    • Stakeholder engagement across languages, faiths and communities
    • Project value communication and beneficiary storytelling
    • Six months of peer webinars, consultation and progress reviews

    National campaign · Radio

    A radio-first grassroots campaign across 774 local governments

    For a national re-election effort, we made the case that the voter who decides elections is listening to radio, in his own language, while he works. The model put trusted local voices on community stations in every local government area and kept them supplied with facts they could defend.

    • Local language programmes in morning, midday and evening slots
    • Recruitment and training of respected community figures as presenters
    • Weekly briefing packs with verified achievements and FAQs
    • Ministry heads on national, state and community radio
    • Call-in formats, fact-checking and counter-narrative protocols
    • Measurement from coverage and call volume to message recall

        About

        Richard Olatunde began in journalism at The Builder Magazine in 2010 and went on to broadcast with UNIQ 103.1 FM from 2012 to 2018, where he learned that a message only matters if the audience hears it. As UNIQ FM's correspondent in Ondo State he was elected Secretary of the NUJ Correspondents' Chapel in 2017. That grounding carried him into government in April 2018 as Special Assistant to the Governor on New Media, where he also presented Media Parley, the state-sponsored radio programme on the work of the Akeredolu administration.

        In 2020 he became the youngest spokesperson of a governorship campaign in Ondo State, and in March 2021 the youngest Chief Press Secretary in the state's history, a post he held until December 2023.

        In that role he managed the government's voice through the Owo church attack, the debate over Amotekun and state policing, and the long months of the governor's illness. He resigned after Governor Akeredolu's death. He is now Lead Strategist at Veritas-Eagles Communications, where he leads work for technology firms, corporate service providers, universities, public sector communicators and campaigns.
